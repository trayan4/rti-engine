# Compensation Policy — Meridian Group

**Document type:** Internal policy (synthetic)
**Version:** 2.1
**Effective:** 1 January 2026
**Applies to:** All employees of Meridian Group in Germany, France and Spain

> This is a fictional employer's policy, created to exercise a compliance
> system against a realistic internal document. Meridian Group does not
> exist. The Directive and national law referenced elsewhere in this
> corpus are real.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how Meridian Group determines pay, how pay
progresses, and how employees may obtain information about their own pay
and about comparable roles. It applies to all employees on permanent and
fixed-term contracts, full time and part time.

Meridian Group employs approximately 6,000 people across Germany, France
and Spain.

## 2. Job architecture

Every role is assigned to one job family and one level. These two
attributes, together with country, determine the pay range for the role.

**Job families**

| Family | Scope |
|---|---|
| Sales | Revenue generation, account management, partnerships |
| Engineering | Software, platform, data and infrastructure roles |
| Marketing | Brand, demand generation, product marketing |
| Legal | Legal counsel, regulatory affairs, contracts |
| Operations | Supply chain, facilities, business operations, support |

**Levels**

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| L1 | Entry. Works to defined instruction. |
| L2 | Developing. Owns defined tasks with guidance. |
| L3 | Established. Owns outcomes independently. |
| L4 | Senior. Sets direction within a function; may lead others. |
| L5 | Principal or executive. Sets direction across functions. |

Levels are assigned against written criteria covering skills,
responsibility, effort and working conditions. The criteria are applied
without reference to the sex of the current or prospective holder.

## 3. Pay components

**Base salary (full-time equivalent).** The annual salary payable for
full-time work in the role. All internal comparison and reporting uses
this figure.

**Base salary (actual).** Base salary as paid, being the full-time
equivalent figure multiplied by the employee's contracted working
fraction.

**Variable pay.** An annual bonus expressed as a percentage of actual
base salary. The target is 12% of base salary. Actual awards vary with
company and individual performance and are not guaranteed.

**Total compensation.** Actual base salary plus variable pay.

## 4. How base pay is set

Base pay is built from four factors, applied in order:

1. **Level anchor.** Each level carries a reference salary, reviewed
   annually against market data.

   | Level | Reference (EUR, full-time) |
   |---|---|
   | L1 | 40,000 |
   | L2 | 55,000 |
   | L3 | 72,000 |
   | L4 | 95,000 |
   | L5 | 130,000 |

2. **Country differential.** Applied to reflect labour market conditions.

   | Country | Differential |
   |---|---|
   | Germany | reference |
   | France | 5% below reference |
   | Spain | 20% below reference |

3. **Job family differential.**

   | Family | Differential |
   |---|---|
   | Engineering | 10% above reference |
   | Legal | 5% above reference |
   | Sales | reference |
   | Marketing | 5% below reference |
   | Operations | 10% below reference |

4. **Length of service.** An uplift of approximately 1.5% of base salary
   for each completed year of service with Meridian Group, reflecting
   accumulated role-specific knowledge.

Individual pay may sit above or below the resulting figure where
justified by prior experience, scarce skills, or a documented retention
case. Any such deviation must be recorded with its reason at the time it
is made.

**Sex is not a factor in any part of this calculation, and no step of it
takes sex as an input.**

## 5. Pay progression

Pay is reviewed annually. Progression arises from:

- **Level advancement**, against the published level criteria.
- **Length of service**, applied automatically as above.
- **Performance**, rated annually on a five-point scale, which
  determines the size of the discretionary element of any increase.

Employees absent on parental or family leave during a review cycle
receive any general increase applied to their level and country, and
their performance rating is based on the period actually worked.

## 6. Working patterns

Part-time employees are contracted at a defined working fraction, most
commonly 0.6 of full time. Part-time employees receive the same
full-time equivalent base salary as comparable full-time employees; their
actual pay is proportionate to hours worked.

**All pay comparison, internal or external, is conducted on a full-time
equivalent basis.** Comparing actual paid amounts across employees with
different working patterns produces a difference that reflects hours
rather than pay, and is not a valid basis for any conclusion about equal
pay.

## 7. Categories of work of equal value

For the purposes of pay comparison, employees are grouped into categories
performing the same work or work of equal value. A category is defined by
the combination of:

- country,
- job family, and
- level.

Categories are constructed from the job evaluation criteria and are
objective and gender-neutral. Where a category contains too few employees
of either sex to permit meaningful comparison, it is not reported on
individually; the minimum group size for reporting is ten employees of
each sex.

## 8. Employee right to pay information

Any employee may request information about their own pay and about
average pay levels, broken down by sex, for the category of employees
performing the same work or work of equal value to theirs.

**Process**

1. Requests are submitted to People Operations, in writing or through an
   employee representative.
2. Receipt is acknowledged within five working days.
3. A substantive response is provided within two months of the request.
4. Where the information provided is incomplete or inaccurate, the
   employee may request clarification, which is provided within one month.

**What is disclosed**

- The employee's own base salary, working fraction, bonus and total
  compensation.
- For the employee's category: average base salary broken down by sex,
  the number of employees of each sex, and the resulting gap.
- The criteria used to determine pay and pay progression.

**What is not disclosed**

- The pay of any identified or identifiable individual other than the
  requester.
- Category figures where the group is below the minimum size in section 7.

Employees may not be prevented from disclosing their own pay, and no
contractual term purporting to do so has effect.

## 9. Pay gap monitoring

People Operations calculates, at least annually:

- the mean and median gap in base salary between female and male
  employees,
- the same gap in total compensation,
- the same gaps by category as defined in section 7,
- the proportion of employees of each sex receiving variable pay.

Where a gap in a category exceeds **5%** and cannot be justified by
objective, gender-neutral criteria, Meridian Group conducts a joint pay
assessment with employee representatives, identifies the causes, and
adopts remedial measures.

A gap that is fully explained by level, job family, country or length of
service is documented as such and does not require remediation. A gap
that cannot be shown to be explained, and cannot be shown to be
statistically reliable in the group concerned, is recorded for monitoring
and reviewed at the next cycle.

## 10. Recruitment

- Applicants are informed of the pay range for the role, before any pay
  discussion takes place.
- Applicants are not asked about current or previous pay.
- Job titles and descriptions are gender-neutral.

## 11. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| People Operations | Maintains this policy, responds to information requests, produces gap reporting |
| Line managers | Apply the pay-setting criteria; record the reason for any deviation |
| Legal | Confirms alignment with EU and national law in each jurisdiction |
| Employee representatives | Consulted on job evaluation criteria and any joint pay assessment |

## 12. Review

This policy is reviewed annually, and additionally whenever national
implementing legislation is adopted in Germany, France or Spain.
