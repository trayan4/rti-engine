# National implementation status — France

**Instrument:** Directive (EU) 2023/970 (EU Pay Transparency Directive)
**Status as at:** 27 July 2026
**Jurisdiction:** France (FR)

## Transposition status

France has not completed transposition. The 7 June 2026 deadline passed
without implementing law in force. A draft law has been published, with
entry into force targeted for 1 January 2027.

France is further advanced than Member States with no published text, but
the draft is not law and creates no obligations until enacted.

## What is currently in force

The applicable national framework is the professional equality index
(Index de l'égalité professionnelle), in force since 2019:

- Applies to employers with 50 or more employees.
- Produces a composite score out of 100 across weighted indicators,
  including the pay gap, the distribution of individual pay rises and
  promotions, pay rises on return from maternity leave, and the number of
  women among the ten highest earners.
- The score must be published annually and reported to the labour
  administration and the social and economic committee.
- Employers scoring below a defined threshold must adopt corrective
  measures within a set period, with financial penalties for failure.

The index differs from the Directive in kind, not only in degree. It is a
composite score rather than a set of category-level gap disclosures, and
it carries no individual right to request comparator pay information.

## Effect of the Directive in the absence of transposition

Private-sector employers are governed by the index regime. The
Directive's provisions are not yet directly binding on them.

Public-sector employers and emanations of the state may be subject to
vertical direct effect from 8 June 2026 in respect of sufficiently clear
and unconditional provisions.

## Practical consequences for a request received now

- There is no statutory individual right to comparator pay information
  equivalent to Article 7 of the Directive.
- Employers may respond voluntarily, but should not present the response
  as compliance with an obligation that is not yet in force in France.
- Category-level gap figures prepared on the Directive's basis are not the
  same as the index score and should not be described as such.

## Caution

This note reflects the position on the date stated above and will change,
particularly as the draft law progresses. Verify against primary sources
before relying on it.
