# National implementation status — Spain

**Instrument:** Directive (EU) 2023/970 (EU Pay Transparency Directive)
**Status as at:** 27 July 2026
**Jurisdiction:** Spain (ES)

## Transposition status

Spain has not transposed the Directive. The 7 June 2026 deadline passed
without implementing legislation. A prior public consultation on a Royal
Decree closed on 8 May 2026, but no draft text had been published as of
early June 2026.

Spain is therefore exposed to infringement proceedings under Article 258
TFEU.

## What is currently in force

Spain has a substantial pre-existing equal pay framework, principally
Royal Decree 902/2020 on equal pay between women and men:

- Every employer, regardless of size, must maintain a pay register
  (registro retributivo) recording average values and pay ranges by sex
  for each professional group, category, level and post of equal value.
- Employees may access the register through their legal representatives.
- Employers with 50 or more employees must conduct a pay audit
  (auditoría retributiva) as part of their equality plan, including a job
  evaluation and an action plan where disparities are found.
- Where the average pay of one sex exceeds the other by 25% or more,
  the employer must justify that the difference is unrelated to sex.

Spain's existing regime is closer to the Directive than most Member
States', but the thresholds, the disclosure mechanism and the trigger for
corrective action all differ.

## Effect of the Directive in the absence of transposition

Private-sector employers are governed by Royal Decree 902/2020 and the
Workers' Statute. The Directive's provisions are not yet directly binding
on them.

Public-sector employers may be subject to vertical direct effect from
8 June 2026 in respect of sufficiently clear and unconditional provisions.

## Practical consequences for a request received now

- Access to pay information runs through the pay register and workers'
  representatives, rather than through an individual right of the kind in
  Article 7 of the Directive.
- The 25% justification threshold in Spanish law is not the Directive's 5%
  joint pay assessment trigger, and the two must not be conflated. A gap
  above 5% but below 25% triggers no obligation under current Spanish law.
- An employer applying the Directive's threshold voluntarily should say
  that it is doing so voluntarily.
